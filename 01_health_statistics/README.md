# Health Statistics — Survey-Based Team Research Project

**Course:** Health Statistics, Ewha Womans University (2024)  
**Project type:** Undergraduate team project (9 members)  
**Tools:** IBM SPSS Statistics  
**Topic:** Perceptions of alternative sweeteners and food purchasing behavior among women in their 20s

## Project Overview

This project explored how perceptions and knowledge of alternative sweeteners were associated with purchasing behavior and purchase intentions among women in their 20s. Our team developed a questionnaire, collected survey responses, and used SPSS to examine relationships between consumer perceptions and purchasing patterns.

I initially proposed the research topic, which was selected through a team vote. I then took an active role in refining the research questions, reviewing background literature, designing the questionnaire, and analyzing categorical data.

## Study Design and Data

- **Design:** Questionnaire-based observational study
- **Participants:** Women in their 20s recruited through convenience sampling
- **Collection period:** September 20–24, 2024
- **Responses:** 137 collected; 135 included in the final analysis after excluding two incomplete responses
- **Survey content:** Perceptions of alternative sweeteners, purchasing patterns, knowledge of positive and negative information, and purchase intentions before and after completing the questionnaire

The survey data were collected for a course project. **Individual-level responses are not publicly shared.**

## Statistical Analysis

The team used SPSS to conduct descriptive statistics and several introductory statistical analyses, including:

- One-way ANOVA with Scheffé post-hoc comparisons
- Pearson correlation analysis
- Linear regression analysis
- Paired-samples t-tests
- Pearson chi-square tests of independence

My main responsibility in the statistical analysis was the **chi-square analysis**, which examined associations between categorical variables related to purchasing behavior.

## Selected Findings

### Purchasing channel and purchase frequency

We compared the reported route through which participants encountered or purchased alternative-sweetener products with their weekly purchase frequency (0–3 vs. 4 or more times).

- **Pearson chi-square statistic:** χ² = 0.854
- **p-value:** 0.837
- **Interpretation:** The analysis did not find statistically significant evidence of an association between the two categorical variables.

Some cells had expected frequencies below five, limiting the reliability of the asymptotic chi-square approximation. This was acknowledged in the final report; an exact or alternative analysis would be worth considering in a follow-up study.

### Purchasing motivation and positive-information scores

A one-way ANOVA found differences in positive-information perception scores across purchasing-motivation groups (**F = 4.896, p = .003**). The report used Scheffé comparisons to examine between-group differences.

This was a team-level analysis and is included to provide an overview of the project's findings, not as a claim of my individual analytical contribution.

## Limitations and Lessons Learned

This was an introductory undergraduate research project. Several limitations are important when interpreting the results:

- **Convenience sampling:** The sample is not representative of all women in their 20s, limiting generalizability.
- **Observational data:** Associations in the survey do not establish causal effects on purchasing behavior.
- **Sparse contingency tables:** Some expected cell counts were below five, making certain chi-square results less reliable.
- **Self-reported responses:** Purchase frequency and perceptions may be subject to reporting and measurement error.

The project gave me experience connecting a research question to questionnaire design, data analysis, and interpretation. It also helped me recognize the importance of checking whether the assumptions behind a statistical test are appropriate for the collected data.
