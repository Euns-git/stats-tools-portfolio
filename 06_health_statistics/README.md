# Health Statistics: Survey-Based Team Project

Team project (9 members) for the Health Statistics course, Ewha Womans University, 2024. Analysis in IBM SPSS Statistics.

**Topic.** Perceptions of alternative sweeteners and food purchasing behavior among women in their 20s.

## Project Overview

The team developed a questionnaire, collected survey responses, and used SPSS to examine how perceptions and knowledge of alternative sweeteners relate to purchasing behavior and purchase intentions. Individual-level responses are not shared.

| Item | Description |
|---|---|
| Design | Questionnaire-based observational study |
| Participants | Women in their 20s, convenience sampling |
| Collection period | September 20-24, 2024 |
| Sample | 137 responses collected; 135 analyzed after excluding two incomplete responses |
| Survey content | Perceptions of alternative sweeteners, purchasing patterns, knowledge of positive and negative information, purchase intentions before and after the questionnaire |

## Statistical Methods

Descriptive statistics, one-way ANOVA with Scheffe post-hoc comparisons, Pearson correlation, linear regression, paired-samples t-tests, and Pearson chi-square tests of independence.

## Example: Chi-square test of independence

**Problem.** Is the route through which participants encountered or purchased alternative-sweetener products associated with their weekly purchase frequency (0-3 vs. 4 or more times)?

**Approach.** Pearson chi-square test of independence in SPSS.

**Result.** chi-square = 0.854, p = 0.837, so there was no statistically significant evidence of an association. Some cells had expected counts below 5, so an exact test would be preferable in a follow-up analysis.

For context, a team-level one-way ANOVA found differences in positive-information perception scores across purchasing-motivation groups (F = 4.896, p = .003).

Convenience sampling limits generalizability; the data are observational and self-reported; some contingency tables had sparse cells.
